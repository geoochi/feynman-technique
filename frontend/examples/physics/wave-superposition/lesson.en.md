# Why do two waves reinforce or cancel?

Two waves travelling in the same direction along the same line add up point by point: at every position the resultant displacement is the algebraic sum of the two individual displacements. Where two crests meet the resultant grows, and where a crest meets a trough it shrinks or cancels completely. This experiment shows y₁ = A₁ sin(kx − ωt), y₂ = A₂ sin(kx − ωt + φ) and their sum y = y₁ + y₂, all animated in time on a shared x-axis.

## Questions
When two waves have equal amplitudes and a phase difference of zero, how many times larger is the resultant amplitude than a single wave?

What happens to the resultant displacement when two waves of equal amplitude have a phase difference of π?

If one of the two amplitudes is set to zero, what is left of the resultant?

## Predict
Write down your prediction first: as the phase difference grows from 0 to π, does the resultant amplitude fall steadily, or does it stay large and only drop sharply near π?

## Try
Press Play and watch the three curves move together in the +x direction. Keep A₁ = A₂ = 1.0 m and φ = 0 and note the resultant amplitude. Then set φ to 180° (π) and watch the sum curve. Finally set A₂ to 0.

## Observe
At φ = 0 the two waves coincide and the resultant amplitude is twice a single wave. At φ = π the two waves are opposite everywhere and the sum stays at zero: they cancel completely. With A₂ = 0 the sum is simply the first wave.

## Explain
### The superposition principle
At any position x and time t the resultant displacement is the algebraic sum of the individual displacements: y = y₁ + y₂. This experiment assumes two waves travelling in the same direction (+x), with the same wavelength and frequency in a linear medium, with no boundaries, no reflections and no damping.

### The resultant amplitude from phasors
Treating A₁ and A₂ as two vectors with an angle φ between them gives the resultant amplitude
A = √(A₁² + A₂² + 2A₁A₂ cos φ).
For φ = 0 this is A₁ + A₂, for φ = π it is |A₁ − A₂|, and when A₁ = A₂ with φ = π it is exactly zero.

### Instantaneous displacement and energy
Superposition says that displacements add; it does not say that energy adds. Where the phase difference is π and the amplitudes are equal, the displacement is always zero. This cancellation is a cancellation of instantaneous displacement, and it should not be read as a claim that energy is destroyed. Energy involves the square of the amplitude and needs a fuller analysis that this experiment does not attempt.

## Check
With A₁ = A₂ = 1.0 m and φ = 0 the resultant amplitude should read 2.0 m; with φ = π the sum curve should lie on zero; and with A₂ = 0 the sum should match the first wave. Compare each case with the readout panel.
