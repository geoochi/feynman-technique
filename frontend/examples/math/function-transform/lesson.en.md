# Quadratic functions

## Questions
How does the vertex move when you change only the horizontal shift h?

How do the sign and magnitude of a affect the parabola's direction and width?

How can you choose a, h, and k to place the vertex at (2, −1) with the parabola opening downward?

## Predict
Before touching the sliders, predict whether changing h from 0 to 2 moves the vertex left or right. Compare your prediction with the result.

## Try
Keep a = 1 and k = 0, and change only h. Then return h to zero and adjust k and a separately. The blue dashed curve always shows y = x²; the amber solid curve shows the current function.

## Observe
h sets the vertex's horizontal position; k sets its vertical position. A larger absolute value of a makes the parabola narrower. Negative a makes it open downward. At a = 0, the graph becomes a horizontal line.

## Explain
The function y = a(x − h)² + k has its vertex at (h, k), except that a = 0 gives no unique vertex. The minus sign inside the brackets means x must equal h for the squared term to vanish. Horizontal shifts change the input; vertical shifts change the output.

## Check
Move the vertex to (2, −1) and make the parabola open downward: h = 2, k = −1, a < 0. Do pairs of points symmetric about x = h have equal function values?

## Explore further
Do the same transformations work for the absolute-value function? For any function f, y = a f(x − h) + k follows similar rules for translation and vertical scaling.
